| **Clicks feel slow in multiplayer** (in progress: [responsiveness.md](responsiveness.md), Q10) | owner, 2026-10-25, while playing | A small delay after each click (moves, actions) in co-op, not in single player. **Likely cause:** the lockstep. A human command doesn't run at once; it is sent to everyone and runs in a later game step (segment), when all PCs have it. So each click waits for a segment boundary and the network. Our co-op guard already halves the segments (200 to 100 ms) when every PC agrees, so check the log says `co-op timing: ON`. **Probe:** with the robot (`--trace-moves`, `--trace-mp`), measure click to execution on two PCs and on one; find how many segments ahead a command is scheduled (the transceiver's send, 0x007c9810, and its pacing, 0x007c8dd0, are decompiled now); check whether that lead can safely shrink when all PCs have our DLL. [../subsystems/multiplayer-sync.md](../subsystems/multiplayer-sync.md) has the segment details. **First data (the owner's log, 2026-10-25):**<ul><li>2 PCs, 2026-10-24: AI moves 0.49 s (90% under 0.53 s).</li><li>4 PCs, 0.1.1, tonight: AI moves 0.90 s (quartiles 0.59 / 1.15 s, 90% under 1.44 s).</li></ul>Co-op timing was ON with 100 ms segments in both, and the AI's own thinking stayed at 1 to 40 ms per move. So with 4 PCs every step waits for the slowest link. **To check:**<ul><li>each player's latency to the others;</li><li>~~whether ZeroTier connects directly or through a relay~~ (the owner: all DIRECT, 2026-10-25);</li><li>a diagnostic setting for real games, logging when each player's "step done" report arrives, so a game log shows who is slow;</li><li>a slow PC.</li></ul>The same wait probably explains the click delay. |
